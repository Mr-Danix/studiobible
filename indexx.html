PART 1: MUSICAL FOUNDATION

CHAPTER 1.1: HARMONY & THEORY

Major Scales

  • Formula: W-W-H-W-W-W-H (Whole, Whole, Half, Whole, Whole, Whole, Half)
  • C Major: 0 sharps/flats | G Major: 1 sharp (F#) | D Major: 2 sharps (F#, C#)
  • A Major: 3 sharps (F#, C#, G#) | E Major: 4 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#)
  • B Major: 5 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#, A#) | F# Major: 6 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#, A#, E#)
  • C# Major: 7 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#, A#, E#, B#) | F Major: 1 flat (Bb)
  • Bb Major: 2 flats (Bb, Eb) | Eb Major: 3 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab)
  • Ab Major: 4 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab, Db) | Db Major: 5 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb)
  • Gb Major: 6 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb, Cb) | Cb Major: 7 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb, Cb, Fb)
  • Circle of Fifths: Memorize completely

Minor Scales — Three Types

  • Natural Minor (Aeolian Mode): Formula: W-H-W-W-H-W-W. Example A: A-B-C-D-E-F-G-A. All 12 natural minor keys.
  • Harmonic Minor: Natural Minor with raised 7th degree. Example A: A-B-C-D-E-F-G#-A. Creates exotic tension, used in jazz and classical. Characteristic: Augmented 2nd interval between 6th and 7th.
  • Melodic Minor: Ascending: Natural Minor with raised 6th and 7th (A-B-C-D-E-F#-G#-A). Descending: Natural Minor (A-G-F-E-D-C-B-A). Used extensively in jazz improvisation.

Pentatonic Scales

  • Major Pentatonic: Formula: 1-2-3-5-6. Example C: C-D-E-G-A. Used in: Country, rock, pop, folk, blues. No half steps — completely consonant.
  • Minor Pentatonic: Formula: 1-b3-4-5-b7. Example A: A-C-D-E-G. Used in: Blues, rock, jazz, pop. The most common scale in popular music.

Blues Scale

  • Formula: 1-b3-4-b5-5-b7. Example A: A-C-D-Eb-E-G.
  • The "blue note" is the b5 (Eb in A). Used in: Blues, rock, jazz, R&B, funk. The foundation of blues-based improvisation.

Triads — Four Types

  • Major Triad: 1-3-5. Sound: Happy, bright, resolved. Example C: C-E-G. Intervals: Major 3rd + Minor 3rd.
  • Minor Triad: 1-b3-5. Sound: Sad, dark, melancholic. Example C: C-Eb-G. Intervals: Minor 3rd + Major 3rd.
  • Diminished Triad: 1-b3-b5. Sound: Tense, unstable, dissonant. Example C: C-Eb-Gb. Intervals: Minor 3rd + Minor 3rd (symmetrical).
  • Augmented Triad: 1-3-#5. Sound: Dreamy, mysterious, unresolved. Example C: C-E-G#. Intervals: Major 3rd + Major 3rd (symmetrical).

7th Chords — Five Types

  • Major 7th (Maj7): 1-3-5-7. Sound: Dreamy, lush, sophisticated. Example Cmaj7: C-E-G-B. Used in: Jazz, bossa nova, ballads.
  • Dominant 7th (Dom7): 1-3-5-b7. Sound: Bluesy, functional, wants to resolve. Example C7: C-E-G-Bb. Used in: Blues, jazz, rock, funk. The most important chord in functional harmony.
  • Minor 7th (Min7): 1-b3-5-b7. Sound: Mellow, modal, smooth. Example Cm7: C-Eb-G-Bb. Used in: Jazz, R&B, funk, pop.
  • Half-Diminished 7th (m7b5): 1-b3-b5-b7. Sound: Dark, tense, often functions as ii in minor. Example Cm7b5: C-Eb-Gb-Bb. Used in: Jazz, classical, film scores.
  • Diminished 7th (Dim7): 1-b3-b5-bb7. Sound: Very tense, symmetrical, modulating. Example Cdim7: C-Eb-Gb-Bbb (A). Used in: Jazz, classical, film scores.

Extensions & Alterations

  • 9ths: Maj9 (1-3-5-7-9, lush), Min9 (1-b3-5-b7-9, smooth), Dom9 (1-3-5-b7-9, bluesy).
  • 11ths: Maj11 (very extended, rare), Min11 (modal, modern jazz), Dom11 (fusion, modern).
  • 13ths: Dom13 (full, rich, complete), Maj13 (lush, cinematic).
  • Alterations: #5 (tense, outside), b9 (dark, Spanish), #9 (Hendrix chord), #11 (Lydian dominant), b13 (altered, outside).

Diatonic Harmony & Functions

  • I (maj7) Tonic | ii (min7) Subdominant | iii (min7) Tonic | IV (maj7) Subdominant | V (dom7) Dominant | vi (min7) Tonic | vii° (half-dim7) Dominant.
  • Tonic Function: I, iii, vi — stable, resolved, home.
  • Subdominant Function: ii, IV — moving away from home.
  • Dominant Function: V, vii° — tension, wants to resolve.
  • Cadences: Authentic (V-I), Plagal (IV-I), Half (ends on V), Deceptive (V-vi).

Nashville Number System

  • Numbers 1-7 represent scale degrees. 1 = Tonic (I), 2 = Supertonic (ii), etc.
  • Example: 1-4-5 in G = G-C-D. 1-6-4-5 in C = C-Am-F-G. 2-5-1 in any key = ii-V-I.
  • Used by session musicians worldwide. Allows instant transposition.

Common Progressions

  • 12-Bar Blues: 1-1-1-1 / 4-4-1-1 / 5-4-1-5 (or 1). Quick Change: 1-4-1-1. Jazz Blues: 1-4-1-1 / 4-#4°-1-1 / 2-5-1-5.
  • ii-V-I: Most important jazz progression. In C: Dm7-G7-Cmaj7. Learn in all 12 keys.
  • I-IV-V: Rock and pop foundation. In C: C-F-G.
  • I-vi-IV-V: The "50s progression" / Doo-wop. In C: C-Am-F-G.
  • Rhythm Changes: Based on "I Got Rhythm". A: I-vi-ii-V / iii-vi-ii-V. Bridge: III7-VI7-II7-V7.
  • Modal Vamps: Two chords only (e.g., Dm7-G7). Static harmony for improvisation.

Modes — Seven Modes of the Major Scale

  • Ionian (I): 1-2-3-4-5-6-7. Bright, happy. Same as major scale.
  • Dorian (ii): 1-2-b3-4-5-6-b7. Minor with raised 6th, jazzy, Santana. Example: All Blues by Miles Davis.
  • Phrygian (iii): 1-b2-b3-4-5-b6-b7. Dark, Spanish, flamenco. Characteristic: b2.
  • Lydian (IV): 1-2-3-#4-5-6-7. Dreamy, sci-fi, bright. Characteristic: #4. Example: The Simpsons theme.
  • Mixolydian (V): 1-2-3-4-5-6-b7. Bluesy, rock, funky. Characteristic: b7. Example: Sweet Child O' Mine.
  • Aeolian (vi): 1-2-b3-4-5-b6-b7. Sad, pure minor. Same as natural minor.
  • Locrian (vii°): 1-b2-b3-4-b5-b6-b7. Unstable, diminished, rarely used. Characteristic: b5.

CHAPTER 1.2: RHYTHM & TIMING

Subdivisions

  • Whole Note: 4 beats in 4/4. Longest common note value.
  • Half Note: 2 beats in 4/4. Two per measure.
  • Quarter Note: 1 beat in 4/4. The pulse, the foundation.
  • Eighth Note: 1/2 beat in 4/4. 8 per measure. Straight or swung.
  • Sixteenth Note: 1/4 beat in 4/4. 16 per measure. Fast, intricate.
  • Triplets: 3 notes in the space of 2. Feel: uneven, rolling. Used in blues shuffle, jazz.
  • Sextuplets: 6 notes in the space of 2. Fast, intricate. Used in classical, prog, fusion.

Swing vs. Straight

  • Swing: First eighth longer, second shorter (like a shuffle). Jazz, blues, funk.
  • Straight: Eighth notes exactly even. Rock, pop, classical.

Time Signatures

  • 4/4 (Common Time): 4 quarter notes per bar. Most common.
  • 3/4 (Waltz Time): 3 quarter notes per bar. Feel: ONE-two-three.
  • 2/4 (March Time): 2 quarter notes per bar. Feel: ONE-two.
  • 6/8: 6 eighth notes per bar. Feel: Two groups of three. Irish jigs, blues.
  • 9/8: 9 eighth notes per bar. Feel: Three groups of three.
  • 12/8: 12 eighth notes per bar. Feel: Four groups of three. Blues shuffles, gospel.
  • 5/4: 5 quarter notes per bar. Feel: Uneven, 3+2 or 2+3. "Take Five," "Mission: Impossible".
  • 7/8: 7 eighth notes per bar. Feel: Uneven, 2+2+3, 3+2+2, 2+3+2. Balkan, prog rock.
  • 7/4: 7 quarter notes per bar. "Money" by Pink Floyd.
  • 11/8: 11 eighth notes per bar. Complex, advanced. Fusion, prog, world music.

Tempo (BPM) Ranges & Feel

  • Ballad: 60-80 BPM — slow, emotional, breathing.
  • Medium: 90-120 BPM — comfortable, walking pace.
  • Up-tempo: 140-200+ BPM — fast, energetic, driving.
  • Double-time feel: Playing twice as fast but keeping the same tempo.
  • Half-time feel: Playing half as fast — creates space, weight, heavier groove.
  • Behind the Beat: Playing slightly late. Laid back, relaxed. R&B, soul, hip-hop, jazz.
  • On Top of the Beat: Playing slightly early. Driving, urgent, exciting. Rock, punk, pop, funk.
  • In the Pocket: Perfectly locked with the rhythm section. The holy grail of rhythm.

Polyrhythms & Groove

  • 3 against 2: Three notes in the space of two (e.g., triplets over eighth notes).
  • 4 against 3: Four notes in the space of three (e.g., 16th notes over 8th note triplets).
  • 5 against 4: Five notes in the space of four. Advanced fusion/prog.
  • Groove: The interlocking feel between instruments. Serve the groove, lock with bass and drums.

CHAPTER 1.3: MELODY & PHRASING

Singable Lines & Call/Response

  • If you can't hum it, it's not melodic. Greatest melodies are simple and singable ("Somewhere Over the Rainbow," "Yesterday," "Fly Me to the Moon").
  • Call & Response: Musical conversation. Play a phrase (call), then answer it (same, different, higher, lower, variation).

Motivic Development — Six Types

  • 1. Repetition: Play the motif again exactly. Creates familiarity.
  • 2. Sequence: Move the motif up or down by step. Creates momentum.
  • 3. Inversion: Flip the motif upside down. Creates contrast.
  • 4. Retrograde: Play the motif backwards. Creates surprise.
  • 5. Augmentation: Lengthen the note values (twice as slow). Creates space.
  • 6. Diminution: Shorten the note values (twice as fast). Creates energy.
  • 7. Fragmentation: Take a piece of the motif and use it. Creates variety.

Space, Articulation & Dynamics

  • Space: Rests are notes. Silence is musical. Breathe between phrases.
  • Articulation: Legato (smooth, connected), Staccato (short, detached), Accents (emphasized), Slurs (smoothly connected), Bends (changing pitch), Slides (gliding), Vibrato (pitch oscillation), Mutes (changes timbre).
  • Dynamics: pp (very soft), p (soft), mp (medium soft), mf (medium loud), f (loud), ff (very loud), Crescendo (gradually louder), Diminuendo (gradually softer).
  • Tone: Your unique sound. Warm vs. bright, dark vs. edgy, mellow vs. aggressive.

CHAPTER 1.4: EAR TRAINING

  • Relative Pitch: The ability to hear a chord or note and instantly know your options. You don't need perfect pitch.
  • Interval Recognition: Hear any interval and name it (ascending, descending, harmonic). Practice daily.
  • Chord Recognition: Hear a chord and name its quality (Major, minor, diminished, augmented, 7th, Maj9, Dom13, etc.).
  • Progression Recognition: Hear a chord progression and identify the chords (ii-V-I, 12-bar blues, I-vi-IV-V).
  • Transcription: Learn solos, melodies, and chord changes by ear. No sheet music. Start with simple melodies, then solos. Transcribe daily.
  • Intonation: Playing in tune. Equal temperament (piano) vs. just intonation (pure intervals). Learn to hear beats (interference patterns) and tune them out.

PART 2: SOUND SYSTEM

CHAPTER 2.1: SIGNAL CHAIN

  1. Source → Musician & Instrument: The starting point. Your playing, technique. Bad source = bad sound.
  2. Transducer → Microphone or DI Box: Converts acoustic sound into electrical signal.
    • Dynamic: SM58, durable, good for loud sources.
    • Condenser: Sensitive, detailed, needs phantom power.
    • Ribbon: Warm, vintage, fragile.
    • DI Box: Converts high-impedance to low-impedance balanced. Active (needs power) vs Passive (transformer-based, no power).
  3. Preamp → Microphone Preamp: Boosts low-level mic signal (millivolts) to usable "line level" (1-2 volts). First place you add gain. Tube preamps color the sound; solid-state are clean. Gain staging: loud but not clipping.
  4. Mixing Console / Interface: Where you mix, route, and process. Mixer combines signals (live). Audio Interface converts analog to digital for DAW (heart of recording setup).
  5. Processor → EQ, Compressor, FX: Shapes and enhances. EQ adjusts frequency balance. Compressor controls dynamics. Effects add space. Gate reduces bleed.
  6. Amplifier → Power Amp: Boosts line-level to speaker-level (100s to 1000s of watts). Match impedance (4, 8, or 16 ohms) for maximum power transfer.
  7. Speaker → Monitor or PA: Converts electrical signal back to sound. Active (built-in amps) vs Passive (external amps). Full-range, Subwoofer (20-150Hz), Tweeter (highs). Monitors: accurate, flat. PA: louder, forgiving.

Key Rules

  • Use balanced cables (XLR/TRS) for long runs (>6m) — they reject noise.
  • Use unbalanced cables (TS) for short runs only.
  • SpeakON for speaker connections — never use instrument cables for speakers (they'll overheat and fail).
  • Patch cables (short TS) for effects pedals.
  • Keep power cables away from audio cables to avoid hum.
  • Label your cables for easy troubleshooting.

CHAPTER 2.3: CRITICAL CONCEPTS

Gain Staging

  • Managing volume levels at every stage. Goal: Maximum signal-to-noise ratio without clipping.
  • Rule: Keep peaks around -6dB to -3dB on meters. Master should never go above 0dB.
  • Unity Gain: Output level equals input level. Start there, then adjust.
  • Headroom: Space between peak level and 0dB. Leave 6-12dB for transients.
  • Digital Clipping: Signal exceeds 0dB, creating square waves — harsh distortion. Avoid at all costs.

Phase

  • When two identical signals are reversed (180 degrees), they cancel out (thinning, losing punch).
  • Causes: Mic placement (different distances), incorrect wiring (pin 2/3 reversed), processing plugins.
  • Solution: Flip the phase on one channel. Listen — if it gets fuller, that's correct. If thinner, flip back.

Feedback & Crossovers

  • Feedback: Mic picks up speaker output (infinite loop). Fix: 1. Mic placement (cardioid pattern). 2. EQ (cut offending freq 1-4kHz). 3. Volume (turn down monitors). 4. Ringing out (increase gain until feedback, cut freq, repeat).
  • Crossovers: Electronic filters splitting signal into bands. Low (20-150Hz) to subwoofer, Mid/High (150Hz-20kHz) to mains. Active (electronic, pre-amp) vs Passive (in speaker, post-amp). Slopes: 12dB, 18dB, 24dB/octave.

PART 3: REAPER & MIXING

CHAPTER 3.1: SETUP & PREFERENCES

Audio Device Settings

  • Options > Preferences > Audio > Device.
  • Driver: Windows: ASIO (lowest latency). Mac: CoreAudio.
  • Buffer Size: Recording: 64-256 samples. Mixing: 512-1024 samples. Mastering: 2048 samples.
  • Sample Rate: 44.1kHz (CD), 48kHz (video), 96kHz (optional).
  • Bit Depth: 24-bit for recording/mixing (optimal), 16-bit for CD release (dithered).

Project Settings & Customization

  • Recording format: WAV (24-bit) — uncompressed, highest quality.
  • Render format: WAV (24-bit) for mastering, MP3 (320kbps) for sharing, FLAC for archiving.
  • Timebase: Beats (position) for most projects (stretches with BPM).
  • Key Commands: "R" record, "Space" play/stop, "Ctrl+S" save, "Shift+R" render.
  • Track Templates: Save "Vocal Chain", "Drum Bus", "Guitar" templates.

Monitoring

  • Direct monitoring: Zero latency, no effects. Use when latency is critical.
  • Through REAPER: With effects, higher latency. Use for mixing.

CHAPTER 3.2: MIXING PROCESS (8 STEPS)

Step 1: Level & Pan

  • Levels: Start faders at unity (0dB). Peaks at -6dB to -3dB. Master never exceeds 0dB.
  • Panning: Center: Kick, bass, snare, lead vocal. L/R 100%: Guitars, overheads. Near center (20-50%): Piano, strings, BG vocals. Creates 3D soundstage.

Step 2: Subtractive EQ

  • HPF: Roll off lows on everything except kick and bass. Vocals: 80-120Hz, Guitars: 80-100Hz, Snare: 80Hz, Overheads: 100-150Hz, Piano: 80-100Hz.
  • Narrow Cuts: Boost narrow Q and sweep to find ugly freqs, then cut with wide Q. Problem areas: 200-400Hz (mud), 1-3kHz (nasal), 4-8kHz (sibilance).
  • Rule: Cut first, boost later.

Step 3: Compression

  • Parallel Compression: Blend compressed and uncompressed signals. Send to compressor (8:1, fast attack/release), mix 30-50% with dry.

Step 4: Additive EQ

  • Low Shelf: Boost warmth/sub-bass. 60-120Hz, +2 to +6dB, Q: 1.0.
  • High Shelf: Boost presence/air. 8-12kHz, +2 to +6dB, Q: 1.0.
  • Peak Boost: Add bite/punch. Varies by instrument, +2 to +6dB, Q: 1.5 to 2.5 (wide for musical results).
  • Rule: Boost less than 6dB. Narrow Q boosts sound unnatural.

Step 5: Effects (Reverb & Delay)

  • Use Sends/Auxiliary Tracks. Don't put reverb directly on the track.
  • Reverb: Room Size (Small 1-5m² to Large 50-100m²), Decay Time (1-2s clear, 3-5s atmospheric), Pre-delay (10-40ms keeps dry clear), Mix (10-30% wet).
  • Delay: Time (1/4, 1/8, or dotted 8th for ping-pong), Feedback (20-40%), Mix (10-20% wet).
  • EQ Your Reverb: Roll off lows below 200Hz (mud) and highs above 8kHz (harshness).

Step 6: Bussing & Group Processing

  • Drum Bus: Glue compressor (2:1, slow attack 30ms, fast release 50ms). Adds cohesion.
  • Vocal Bus: Compress together for unified sound.
  • Guitar Bus: EQ together, cut 300-400Hz for clarity.
  • Instrument Bus: Light compression for glue. Saves CPU.

Step 7: Automation

  • Volume: Ride vocal fader, bring up solos, fade intros/outros, duck background instruments.
  • Pan: Move instruments subtly for motion.
  • FX: Increase reverb on last word, add delay to specific words, automate EQ for sections.
  • Modes: Read (plays back), Write (records real-time, overwrites), Latch (records until stopped, returns to Read), Touch (records only while moving fader).

Step 8: Master Bus Processing

  • Master EQ: Subtle HPF at 30Hz, slight midrange boost (1-3dB). Less is more.
  • Master Compression: 2:1 ratio, slow attack (30ms), fast release (50ms), 1-3dB gain reduction. Glues mix.
  • Limiter: Ceiling at -0.5dB. No more than 2-3dB gain reduction.
  • Stereo Imaging: Slight widening (max 10%). Check mono compatibility.
  • Final Level: Peaks at -6dB to -3dB before mastering. Leave headroom.

CHAPTER 3.3: REAPER SPECIFICS

ReaEQ

  • Band 1: Low Shelf or HPF (LOW). Band 2: Band/Peak (LOW-MID). Band 3: Band/Peak (HIGH-MID). Band 4: High Shelf (HIGH).
  • Use "Band" for cuts/boosts. Q = Bandwidth (lower Q = wider, higher Q = narrower).

ReaComp

  • RMS Size: 5ms (fast response). Auto-makeup: ON. Knee: 3dB (smoother).
  • Start with 3:1 for vocals, 4-5:1 for drums, 5:1 for bass. Adjust threshold for 3-6dB gain reduction.

ReaGate

  • Threshold: Just above bleed level. Attack: 3-5ms. Hold: 15-30ms. Release: 40-80ms. Reduction: -15 to -20dB. Hysteresis: 3-6dB (prevents chatter).
  • Use on drums to reduce bleed, vocals to reduce background noise.

Render Settings

  • Sample Rate: Same as project. Bit Depth: 24-bit for mastering, 16-bit for CD.
  • Dithering: Add when going to 16-bit (prevents digital distortion). Format: WAV for final, MP3 for sharing.

PART 5: PRO TIPS & FINAL CHECKS

5.1: Gain Staging

  • Peaks at -6dB on each track.
  • Master at -3dB max before mastering.
  • Leave 6-12dB headroom for transients.
  • Never let the master hit 0dB (digital clipping).

5.2: Mono Check

  • Sum to mono.
  • If instruments disappear, fix phase issues.
  • Check that kick, snare, bass, and lead vocal are still prominent.
  • Good mono compatibility = better translation on all systems.

5.3: Reference Track

  • A/B against a pro mix in your genre.
  • Use a reference track plugin (e.g., Reference 4, Magic AB).
  • Listen for: Balance, tonal balance, dynamics, stereo width, loudness.

5.4: Check on Multiple Systems

  • Studio monitors: Main listening.
  • Headphones: Detailed listening.
  • Car stereo: Real-world test.
  • Phone speaker: Mono, lo-fi test.
  • Earbuds: Translation check.
  • Bluetooth speaker: Consumer test.

5.5: Take Breaks

  • Ears fatigue after 45-60 minutes.
  • Take 5-10 minute breaks every hour.
  • Come back with fresh ears.
  • A/B compare with fresh ears.

5.6: Trust Your Ears

  • Don't mix with your eyes.
  • Use your ears, not the meters (but meters help).
  • If it sounds good, it is good.
  • Don't over-process. Less is usually more.
